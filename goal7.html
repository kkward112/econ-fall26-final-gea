The goal

Goal 7: Affordable and Clean Energy

“Ensure access to affordable, reliable, sustainable and modern energy for all.” — one of 17 goals adopted by every UN member state in 2015 [1].

What does it actually mean?

Goal 7 is about more than switching to renewables. It asks for four things at once: energy that people can afford, that is reliable enough to depend on, that is sustainable for the planet, and that is modern — electricity and clean cooking rather than open fires.

Energy is often called the “golden thread” of development because it connects to health, education, gender equality, jobs and climate action.

Who is left behind?

Around 80% of people without electricity live in sub-Saharan Africa, many in rural areas far from the grid [2]. Women and girls often spend hours each week collecting firewood for cooking.

The five targets

Official SDG 7 targets (summarised from the UN [1])
TargetWhat it aims for
7.1By 2030, ensure universal access to affordable, reliable and modern energy services.
7.2By 2030, increase substantially the share of renewable energy in the global energy mix.
7.3By 2030, double the global rate of improvement in energy efficiency.
7.aBy 2030, enhance international cooperation to facilitate access to clean energy research and technology, and promote investment in energy infrastructure.
7.bBy 2030, expand infrastructure and upgrade technology for supplying modern and sustainable energy services in developing countries.

Where are we now?

⚡ Electricity access

Global access reached about 91% in 2022, but the number of people without power rose for the first time in a decade, to 685 million [2].

🔥 Clean cooking

About 74% of people have clean cooking, leaving 2.1 billion relying on polluting fuels [2].

🌱 Renewables

Renewables supplied under one-fifth of total final energy consumption in 2021 — far below what is needed [2].

📉 Efficiency

Efficiency is improving at under half the 2.6% annual rate the target requires [2].

On current trends, the world is not on track to meet Goal 7 by 2030 [1] [2].
