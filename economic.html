Economics

Economic impact

Clean energy used to be the expensive option. Today it is often the cheapest — and energy access unlocks income, productivity and jobs.

Renewables have become cheaper

According to IRENA, the global average cost of electricity from new solar PV fell by about 90% between 2010 and 2023, and around 81% of new renewable capacity added in 2023 produced power more cheaply than fossil-fuel alternatives [3].

What this means for households

Families using kerosene lamps can spend a significant share of their income on fuel. A small solar kit, paid in instalments, can cost less over time while giving brighter, safer light.

Productive use

Reliable power lets farmers run irrigation pumps and grain mills, tailors use electric machines and shops stay open after dark — turning energy into income.

Investment gap

Clean energy investment is growing fast but remains concentrated in advanced economies and China; developing economies need far more finance [4].

Is solar right for your Florida home? An honest checklist

✅ It may be a good fit if…

  • Your roof gets strong sun and has few shade trees
  • Your roof is in good condition and won’t need replacing soon
  • You plan to stay in the home for many years
  • Your electricity bills are high and keep rising

⚠️ Think carefully if…

  • Your roof is shaded, small, or nearing the end of its life
  • You may move soon
  • A loan or lease has fees that cancel out the savings
  • Your HOA or utility has rules that limit installation or credits

🌀 Hurricane reality check

Panels on their own usually shut off during a grid outage for safety reasons. If backup power during storms matters to you, ask specifically about battery storage and whether the system can run while the grid is down.

The cost of doing nothing

Energy poverty has hidden costs: illness and early deaths from smoky kitchens [5], lost school and work hours, spoiled food and medicines, and exposure to volatile fossil-fuel prices. Investing in Goal 7 is therefore not only an environmental choice — it is an economic one.
