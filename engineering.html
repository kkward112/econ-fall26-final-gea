Engineering

Engineering solutions

Engineers are not waiting for one big breakthrough — many proven technologies already exist. The challenge is designing them to be affordable, durable and right for local conditions.

Key technologies

☀️

Solar home systems & mini-grids

A rooftop panel, battery and LED lights can power a home far from the grid. Mini-grids link panels and batteries to serve a whole village, often cheaper and faster than extending national power lines to remote areas [4].

Best for: Remote and rural communities

🌬️

Wind power

Turbines convert moving air into electricity. Onshore wind is now one of the cheapest sources of new power in many countries [3].

Best for: Windy coastal and open regions

🔋

Battery storage

Sun and wind vary. Lithium-ion and emerging chemistries store surplus power for evening use, making renewables reliable. Battery costs have fallen sharply over the last decade [4].

Best for: Grids with lots of solar or wind

🍲

Clean cookstoves

Improved biomass, LPG, biogas and electric induction stoves cut smoke indoors and reduce firewood use, directly tackling household air pollution [5].

Best for: Households cooking on open fires

💧

Small hydropower

Run-of-river turbines generate steady power from streams without large dams, suiting hilly areas.

Best for: Mountain and river valley communities

🏠

Energy efficiency

LED lighting, insulation, efficient motors and smart meters deliver the same service with less energy — often the cheapest “source” of energy of all [4].

Best for: Everywhere

Case study: a solar mini-grid

Picture a rural village 40 km from the nearest power line. Extending the grid could take years. Instead, engineers install:

  1. Solar arrays sized to the village’s daytime demand.
  2. A battery bank to supply lights, phones and fridges after dark.
  3. An inverter and local distribution lines to deliver power to homes, shops and a clinic.
  4. Pay-as-you-go smart meters so families buy only what they can afford, often by mobile money.

The World Bank and IEA identify mini-grids and stand-alone solar as the least-cost way to connect a large share of people still without power [2] [4].

Design challenges

  • Dust, heat and humidity shorten equipment life
  • Local technicians must be trained for repairs
  • Batteries need responsible recycling
  • Systems must grow as demand grows
